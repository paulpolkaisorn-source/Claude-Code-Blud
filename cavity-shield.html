Why water saves your teeth

  • Water washes sugar and food bits off your teeth.
  • It keeps your mouth full of spit (saliva), which is your body's own tooth repair liquid.
  • Tap water usually has fluoride, which makes enamel harder for acid to dig into.
  • Drink a few sips after every snack or juice. It's like a mini rinse.
  • After your night brush, the only drink allowed is plain water.

Easy tricks: keep a bottle on your desk, drink one glass with every meal, one when you get home, and one before each screen session.

The 6 golden food rules

1

Sweets with meals, not between. A dessert right after lunch is one acid attack. The same candy spread over the afternoon is five.

2

Don't sip sweet drinks slowly. Juice, soda and milk tea bathe your teeth in sugar the whole time you sip. If you have one, finish it with a meal and use a straw.

3

Finish with a tooth hero. A cube of cheese, plain yogurt or crunchy veggies at the end of a meal pushes your mouth back to safe.

4

Water after every treat. Swish and swallow. It washes sugar away right now.

5

Wait 30 minutes to brush after sour stuff. Acid (oranges, lemon, soda, sour candy) softens enamel for a bit. Brushing right away scrubs the soft layer. Rinse with water first, brush later.

6

Nothing but water after the night brush. Spit makes less at night, so sugar left on teeth does the most damage while you sleep.

Snack box ideas (between meals)

  • Cheese cubes + cucumber sticks
  • Plain Greek yogurt with a few blueberries stirred in
  • Hard-boiled egg + cherry tomatoes
  • Carrot and celery sticks with hummus
  • Edamame with a pinch of salt
  • Turkey or ham roll-ups with cheese
  • Plain popcorn (brush or floss after, the shells stick)
  • A handful of almonds or cashews (if you're not allergic)

Brushing like a pro

  • Tilt the brush toward the gums, small gentle circles. Hard scrubbing hurts gums.
  • Do outside, inside and the chewing tops of every tooth. The back molars get holes the most.
  • Brush your tongue at the end.
  • Floss once a day, best before the night brush. Holes love hiding between teeth where the brush can't reach.
  • New brush every 3 months, or when the bristles splay out.

Real talk about pH water

Alkaline (high pH) water is safe to drink, but it doesn't protect teeth better than normal water. It's back to normal in your mouth within seconds. What actually helps teeth is fluoride, and most bottled and alkaline waters have little or none.

Best for teethPlain tap waterUsually has fluoride. Free. Filter it if you like the taste better (most jug filters keep fluoride; reverse osmosis removes it).
FinepH / alkaline waterOK to drink. No extra tooth power. Check the label for fluoride.
CarefulFlavored sparkling waterNo sugar, but a bit acidic. Have it with meals, not all day.

Ask your parents or dentist whether your home tap water has fluoride. If it doesn't, the dentist may suggest fluoride toothpaste, rinse or varnish.

Infinite budget shopping list

  • Electric toothbrush with a 2-minute timer and pressure sensor.
  • Water flosser or floss picks, so flossing is actually fun.
  • Plaque disclosing tablets. Chew one and plaque turns purple, so you see exactly where you missed.
  • A smart water bottle that glows when it's time to drink, or one with hour lines printed on the side.
  • Kids' fluoride mouthwash (age 6 and up, don't swallow).
  • Xylitol gum or mints (sugar-free) for after meals at school, if you're old enough to chew gum safely.
  • Fancy cheese and fresh veggie snack boxes every week.

Dentist checklist

  • Checkup and cleaning every 6 months.
  • Ask about sealants: a clear coating painted on back teeth that blocks holes. Super worth it.
  • Ask about fluoride varnish at each visit.
  • Tell an adult right away if a tooth hurts with sweet, hot or cold food, or you see a brown or black spot.
