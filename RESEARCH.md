# Research notes behind Forearm Forge

Everything in the app's plan comes from the sources below. Gathered November 2026.

## 1. Who Devon Larratt is

- Canadian pro arm wrestler, born 24 April 1975 in Victoria, British Columbia. Nickname "No Limits".
- About 196 cm (6'5") and 120 kg (265 lb).
- Spent about 16.5 years in Canadian special forces (Joint Task Force 2), deployed seven times to Afghanistan, retired in 2016.
- Got into arm wrestling as an early teen. His first opponent was his grandmother.
- Highlights: beat John Brzenk in 2008; ranked #1 in the world (both arms) in 2010; lost to Denis Cyplenkov 0–6 in 2018 and then rebuilt his strength training; beat Cyplenkov 5–0 in 2023 and won the East vs West super-heavyweight title at 48, the oldest ever to do it.
- His style: can win with almost any move (hook, top roll, press, king's move), beats bigger guys with technique, leverage and hand control, and has huge endurance for long supermatches.
- Source: [Wikipedia: Devon Larratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_Larratt) (the article itself is flagged for weak citations, so treat exact rankings and dates as approximate).
- His exact gym routine is **not** well documented by reliable sources. Most "Devon's training secrets" pages online are low-quality SEO posts. The consistent themes (wrist curls, reverse curls, pronation/supination, grip and thick-bar work, cable/pulley angles, table time) are what the app's forearm block is modeled on, scaled to light, high-rep, teen-safe versions.

## 2. Lifting weights at 13

- The American Academy of Pediatrics (2020 clinical report, *Resistance Training for Children and Adolescents*, Stricker, Faigenbaum & McCambridge, [doi:10.1542/peds.2020-1011](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2020-1011)) says resistance training is safe and beneficial for kids and teens with proper technique and qualified supervision. Readiness depends on experience and maturity more than age.
  - Use dynamic warm-ups and lighter stretching to cool down.
  - Train all major muscle groups, combined with aerobic and skill work.
  - See a doctor first if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure, seizures, certain heart conditions, or a history of chemotherapy.
  - Teens should be counseled about the risks of steroids.
  - Source: [HealthyChildren.org (AAP)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/Guidance-on-Resistance-Training-for-Children.aspx)
- AOSSM STOP Sports Injuries youth strength training tip sheet ([PDF](https://www.sportsmed.org/uploads/main/files/general/Resources/STOP-Tip-Sheets/General/2026-Youth-Strength-Training-EM.pdf)):
  - 2–3 sessions a week on **non-consecutive days**.
  - 5–10 minute warm-up.
  - Start with **one light set of 10–15 reps**, 8–12 exercises covering upper body, lower body and core.
  - Progress to **2–3 sets of 6–15 reps**.
  - Increase resistance gradually. Technique first. Kids should not start strength training on their own. Keep a workout log.

How the app uses this: 3 days a week with a rest day between, 8 exercises per day, the first 3 workouts are 1 set, Phase 1 = 2 × 12–15, Phase 2 = 3 × 10–12, Phase 3 = 3 × 8–12. Forearm work stays light at 15–20 reps. Weight only goes up after hitting the top of the rep range on every set (double progression). No max tests.

## 3. Arm wrestling and teen elbows (the big one)

- A review of 27 studies (68 cases) of the medial epicondyle fracture from arm wrestling found **93% of patients were 13–16 years old**, most often boys aged 14–15. Nearly two in three had no elbow pain beforehand.
- Why: during puberty, muscle strength rises faster than the bone matures. The growth plate on the inside of the elbow is still cartilage turning into bone, and a sudden jerk can pull it apart. Adults get a different injury (a spiral fracture higher up the humerus).
- Advice from the researchers: keep force steady, avoid sudden jerky movements.
- Source: [Science News Explores](https://www.snexplores.org/article/teen-arm-wrestlers-risk-unusual-elbow-break-medial-epicondyle). Supporting review: [Arm-wrestling injuries, systematic review](https://mtprehabjournal.emnuvens.com.br/revista/article/view/906).
- Table-safety habits that show up across arm wrestling guides (gym and federation-style blogs, not medical sources): keep your eyes on your hand, keep your shoulder in line with or behind your hand, never turn your body away from your arm ("break-arm position").

How the app uses this: no all-out table matches for now. Forearm and wrist training is slow, light and high-rep. Every exercise has a "Something hurts" button that explains the warning signs and skips the exercise.

## 4. Eating

- A nitrogen-balance study of male soccer players averaging 13.8 years old estimated a protein requirement of 1.20 g/kg/day and a recommended intake of **1.40 g/kg/day** ([Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/protein-requirements-in-male-adolescent-soccer-players/21066078)). Reviews of teen athletes give 1.2–2.0 g/kg depending on training load, with no benefit above ~3 g/kg.
- How the app uses this: your range is 1.2–1.6 g per kg of body weight, goal = 1.4 g/kg (e.g. 45 kg → 54–72 g, goal 63 g). No calorie counting and no dieting, because a 13-year-old is still growing.
- Calcium: teens 9–18 need 1,300 mg a day (about 3–4 servings of dairy or fortified alternatives).

## 5. Supplements

- Coverage of AAP-linked research reports that the AAP and the American College of Sports Medicine advise **against creatine for anyone under 18**; many products carry "not for minors" labels, and supplement purity isn't guaranteed ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/creatine-bodybuilding-dietary-supplement-easy-for-teens-to-buy), [WGBH](https://wgbh.org/news/2017-01-02/is-the-warning-that-creatines-not-for-teens-getting-through)).
- How the app uses this: no supplements, pre-workouts, fat burners, energy drinks or steroids. Food does the job.

## 6. Sleep

- The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (endorsed by the AAP) recommends **8–10 hours** per night for ages 13–18 ([AASM](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/teen-sleep-duration-recommendation.pdf)).
- How the app uses this: a nightly sleep log with the 8–10 hour target, worth XP.

## What isn't covered

- Arm wrestling technique coaching. That needs a real coach and a table, later.
- Anything medical. If something hurts in a joint, tell a parent and see a doctor.
